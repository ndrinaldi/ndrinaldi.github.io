Two ways in

Why files arrive two different ways

Fast lane

Usenet

  • Feels like a paid, private download from one big server.
  • Speed as fast as the internet line allows.
  • Privacy an encrypted connection to one provider. No VPN needed.
  • Catch not everything is there, especially fan-subtitled anime.

Shared lane

BitTorrent

  • Feels like many people each sending a piece of the file.
  • Speed depends on how many people have it.
  • Privacy everything goes through a VPN. If the VPN drops, it stops instead of leaking.
  • Catch slower for rare titles, and the file is shared back for a while.

The librarian tries the fast lane first. When the index only finds a title on the shared lane, it goes that way instead. You never choose; it just shows up.

Where the file lives new today

A few hours on the box, then the cloud

Local diskThe box's own drive. Small, fast, and only for what just arrived.
CloudStorage rented from Amazon. Grows without buying drives; three copies in three buildings.

Playback is the same either way. The TV never knows which one it is reading from.

Where the assistant fits

Someone is always watching the pipeline

A Claude assistant runs on the box in the house. It is the reason a stuck download usually fixes itself before anyone notices.

Checks health every half hourAre the parts running, is the disk filling up, did a download fail, is the VPN still up.
Unsticks downloads on its ownA bad file, a stalled queue, an episode that never got filed. It clears it and grabs another copy.
Adds shows Nick asks for by text"Grab season 2 of X" becomes a request in the librarian, with the right quality picked.
Answers "why isn't X showing up"It walks the same path this page does and reports the one step where the title got stuck.
How it is reachedFrom Nick's phone, through the Claude app. It runs on the box and talks to the parts directly; the phone is just the remote.

What it deliberately cannot do

  • Touch anything outside the media system. The box's settings, the home network, the router, other accounts. If a fix needs those, it writes a note for Nick instead.
  • Message anyone on its own. It has no way to text or email. Problems it cannot fix wait in an inbox Nick reads.
  • Open the library to the internet. Sharing stays a Plex invite that Nick sends by hand.
  • Turn the VPN off. The torrent app has no network except the tunnel; there is no switch to flip.
  • See your phone. It sees what Plex played and what was requested, nothing else.
